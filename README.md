## Four things to confirm before you pay

**1. Whether the original email comes with it.** A bound mailbox you can't log into is not the same as the original registration email. On PlayerSells, every listing prints whether the original email is included, and its escrow does not release funds until the buyer has moved the account onto an address they control, on the reasoning that without the original email the previous owner keeps a recovery path. One of its listings, a 35.9K-follower account at $150, is sold explicitly without the original email. Accountboy's X page promises the mailbox and its password but not access, so ask support directly before ordering.

**2. The 2FA secret.** Accountboy's X page doesn't mention a two-factor secret at all. PlayerSells treats it as part of a complete handover alongside the password and original email. If a listing is silent on 2FA, plan to set it up yourself after binding your phone.

**3. Age and last activity.** The registration year is printed on some SKUs and the trade-off between them is the main thing you're paying for. Last activity isn't published anywhere in this catalog, so assume a dormant handle and warm it up accordingly.

**4. The exact after-sales terms.** Three marketplaces in this space handle buyer protection in three different ways. Fameseller advertises a 180-day guarantee with a 7-day window to check and swap an account. PlayerSells runs escrow that releases only after a password and email change. Fameswap holds funds through an inspection period. Accountboy's 3-day replacement window for quality issues is the shortest of the four, and it excludes change-of-mind refunds and wrong-item purchases outright. None of these is the industry standard; they're four different contracts.

## What buying an account doesn't get you

X's terms restrict account transfers. PlayerSells puts it plainly in its own FAQ: escrow protects you from the seller, not from the platform's rules, so the handover is something to complete quietly rather than a supported feature. No marketplace changes that, including Accountboy.

The monetization picture moved too. X retired its Creator Revenue Sharing program on September 7, 2026, and the replacement, Original Content Rewards, has its own eligibility rules that a new owner has to meet independently. A purchased handle does not arrive with payouts attached. Most buyers of established accounts monetize through brand deals, affiliate links, newsletter funnels and traffic to their own products, and an existing engaged audience shortens the time to the first of those. It does not guarantee any of them.

## The first week decides whether the handle survives

Accountboy's own usage notes are stricter than most buyers expect, and ignoring them is the most common way a cheap account dies:

- Change the password as soon as you receive the login, along with any other verification method attached to the account.
- Don't touch the nickname, avatar, bio or username on day one. The page says to wait 3 to 7 days and change one field per day.
- Don't follow, retweet or like immediately. Warm the account up for 2 to 3 days, post your first tweet on day 3, keep it to no more than 2 tweets a day, and expect things to settle after 7 days.
- Bind your own phone number before you start posting. The page warns that liking or following without a bound number is a bad idea.
- Log in from a clean environment. Verification-code prompts during login are treated as an environment issue and fall outside after-sales.
- If the account is stolen after delivery, Accountboy states it cannot retrieve it.

One practical note on timing: the site banner promises delivery within 3 minutes, while the product instructions describe automatic delivery within 24 hours. Plan around the longer figure.
